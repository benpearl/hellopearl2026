Abscess

AB-ses

A localized collection of pus caused by bacterial infection, occurring either at the tooth root tip (periapical) or in the gum tissue (periodontal), requiring prompt treatment to prevent spread.

What is an Abscess?

A dental abscess represents a bacterial infection that has formed a pocket of pus. Periapical abscesses originate from pulp necrosis, typically due to untreated decay or trauma. Periodontal abscesses arise from gum disease, often in deep periodontal pockets. Both types can cause significant pain and swelling, and may lead to serious complications if bacteria spread to surrounding tissues or enter the bloodstream. Abscesses do not resolve spontaneously and require professional treatment to eliminate the infection source.

Clinical Significance

Abscesses require urgent attention. Treatment focuses on eliminating the infection source, not just symptom management.

Important:

Dental abscesses are serious infections that require immediate professional treatment. Delaying care can lead to severe complications.

Common Causes

  • Untreated dental caries leading to pulp death
  • Periodontal disease with deep pockets
  • Cracked or fractured teeth
  • Failed root canal treatment
  • Foreign body impaction

Signs & Symptoms

  • Severe throbbing pain
  • Swelling of face or gums
  • Sensitivity to pressure and temperature
  • Fever
  • Bad taste from draining pus
  • Difficulty swallowing or breathing in severe cases

How It's Detected

  • Clinical examination revealing swelling and tenderness
  • Periapical radiograph showing radiolucency
  • Vitality testing of involved tooth
  • Assessment of periodontal pockets

Treatment Options

  • Drainage of abscess
  • Root canal treatment if tooth is restorable
  • Extraction if tooth cannot be saved
  • Antibiotics when infection is spreading
  • Pain management

Prevention

  • Prompt treatment of cavities before pulp involvement
  • Regular periodontal maintenance
  • Protective mouthguards to prevent dental trauma

When to See a Dentist

Seek Immediate Care:

If experiencing facial swelling, fever, or difficulty swallowing. These may indicate spreading infection requiring urgent care.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an abscess go away on its own? ▼

No. An abscess requires treatment to eliminate the infection source. Antibiotics alone do not resolve the underlying problem.

Should I go to the emergency room? ▼

If experiencing difficulty breathing, swallowing, or high fever, seek emergency care immediately. Otherwise, contact your dentist urgently.

Will antibiotics cure my abscess? ▼

Antibiotics help control spreading infection but do not cure an abscess. The infection source must be treated through drainage, root canal, or extraction.
